Setting The Foundation

The Client Home, referred to internally as "Home," was developed as part of a three-phase initiative aimed at addressing the needs of multiple development teams and stakeholders across Compass. This case study focuses on the work completed by the buy-side team and outlines the key deliverables from Phase 1. Based on business priorities, market demand, and the agreed-upon scope of work, the buy-side team was the first and only team to deploy during this initial phase. The work completed here establishes a foundation that other teams will build upon in future phases of the Client Home.

Fragmented Experiences Across Products

Currently within Compass, there are multiple tools that both clients and agents can create and use. Two of the most prominent are "Collections" and "Saved Searches." While both serve important purposes, they operate independently and offer different capabilities depending on who initiates them, client or agent. This separation introduces confusion and inconsistency in the collaborative experience.

For instance, when an agent creates a collection and shares it with a client, the client gains access to a full set of collaborative features. These include favoriting or marking listings as not interested, commenting, sharing listings, and requesting tours. Agents are notified whenever clients interact with these features, allowing for active and informed engagement.

However, when a client creates a collection or saved search, the functionality is much more limited. The only available action is favoriting a listing, and this activity is not visible to the agent. The lack of shared visibility creates a disconnect, hindering collaboration and diminishing the overall experience for both the client and the agent.

Addressing Collaboration Gaps

These usability gaps have been documented by Compass’s research team and reflect larger issues in how clients, agents, and the platform interact. Solving these challenges is a core focus of the Client Home initiative. Phase 1, led by the buy-side team, is specifically designed to close these gaps by establishing a unified, collaborative foundation. This groundwork will support future enhancements and ensure a more cohesive and user-friendly experience across the Compass platform.

Research

User Testing

First Round Research

In the first round of testing, the 'Client Home' and 'Feed' were visibly separate products; each had their own place within the navigation. The hypothesis was that these products had their separate work streams, finding it easier to manage their home search activities.

Research Findings

Jared, from our research team, conducted a moderated, mobile app test, with 8 participants on usertesting.com. The first round of testing takeaways can be seen below.

  • RESEARCH TAKEAWAYS
  • The proposed experience is on the right track, with participants rating it as equal to or better than competitor apps.
    Client Home was understood as a “summary” of the buyer’s home search.Straightforward and did what they believed it should do.
    Participants were unclear about the 'Feed.' They didn’t realize it displayed listing updates rather than all listings or saved ones. It also wasn’t clearly distinct from Client Home or Notifications (which were not covered in this study).
    Triaging options at the listing-card level were generally sufficient, though some participants felt a 'maybe' option could help with narrowing down choices. Others described a two-step review process: first filtering out 'not interested' listings, then ranking the remaining ones into top- and second-tier groups. In that context, a 'maybe' option wasn’t seen as necessary.
    In-app communication was once again viewed as especially valuable and a key differentiator.

Design Iterations & Strategy

After the first round of testing was complete, the buy-side team knew that client home and feed as separate products and work streams on the navbar would not be optimal for our client base.

As I get further into the buy-side client home and feed concepts, I begin to familiarize myself with other teams and stakeholder needs. The cross-functional team collaboration helped to highlight questions and shortcomings of what Client Home could be.

The images below illustrates some of the strategic design conversations that I identified that need understanding from cross-functional teams on how they will execute their products into the client home. Some of the concerns are: how will client home scale in the current IA and with other teams products and services coming post M0 launch, the current IA is lacking alignment based on user feedback and needs realignment across the organization, scenarios regarding multiple transactions; what does an agent get to see and how that might affect data and multiple transactions.

IA Strategy

The IA on the app needs to alignment with all cross-functional stakeholders. The current agreement for M0 deployment, the internal name for the first client home mvp, is the buy-side team will take over the current “Home” nav item for the new client home. All other nav items will stay the same. Duplication of content in the “Saved” nav item become useless and potentially a pain point for users.

Milestone 0 Invite & Flow

I brought up a concern in Compass’s current agent onboarding process that would promote a dark pattern if no solution was in place for Milestone 0. In the current process, an agent sends out an email for the potential client to accept so they can begin working together in a single collaborative space. The concern comes into play when a client is already in the app prior to their relationship or bypasses the email and downloads the app with the email address tied to the agent invite email. The current system does not take these scenarios into account nor when a client has a previous purchase through Compass or multiple active client/agent relationships.

My goal for our current and future clients is to be upfront and honest, while not making assumptions about their needs. Just because an email went out from an agent shouldn’t mean we auto-accept them into the Client Home space.
